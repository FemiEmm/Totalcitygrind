## 1. Animation, player, camera, and traffic freeze

### Problem

Population traffic threw a `ReferenceError` before the animation frame reached
the player movement and camera updates. This made all of the following appear
broken at the same time:

- Civilian traffic
- Player vehicle movement
- Follow camera
- Observation-mode camera
- Game-clock progression

### Work completed

- Restored the missing distinction between regular routes and permanent-loop
  routes.
- Restored permanent-loop traffic counting and creation.
- Protected the main animation frame with a traffic-update error boundary so a
  future traffic exception cannot freeze the entire game.
- Verified that the player movement system advances the vehicle normally and
  that the live game loop and game clock continue running.

## 2. BRT implementation

Two red vehicles labelled **BRT** were added.

### BRT behaviour

- Both BRTs spawn at the central bus terminal.
- They use separate terminal exit roads so they do not spawn on top of each
  other.
- They travel on permanent closed city loops.
- They return to their original terminal positions.
- They wait for 30 in-game minutes after completing a loop.
- The second BRT has a short initial departure delay so both buses do not leave
  simultaneously.
- They are never selected as random civilian vehicle types.
- They use the same ordinary leader, collision, signal, and junction rules as
  civilian traffic.

### Route corrections

The first BRT routes used perimeter segments that intersected highway median
barriers. They were rerouted onto physically validated city corridors.

The routes now pass full vehicle-body checks through every straight segment and
turn, including building, landmark, barrier, and median clearance.

## 3. Civilian vehicle spawning

### Previous problem

The startup seeding system placed vehicles at random progress points inside
their routes. This made cars appear suddenly in the middle of the city.

### Current rule

- There are 15 civilian traffic gateways at the map edges.
- Startup creates at most one ordinary vehicle at each gateway.
- Every later civilian vehicle also begins at route point zero at an edge
  gateway.
- No civilian vehicle is inserted into the middle of a route.
- Additional traffic enters gradually as gateway space becomes available.
- BRTs are the only exception because their declared spawn locations are at the
  bus terminal.

The old midpoint route-geometry seeding code and its unused configuration
values were removed.

## 4. Vehicle teleporting and stacking behind BRTs

### Root cause

Followers treated a BRT approaching a `noSmooth` waypoint as requiring obsolete
roundabout clearance. The referenced
`roundaboutFollowingExtraGap` configuration value did not exist.

That produced the following failure:

1. Following speed became `NaN`.
2. Movement distance became `NaN`.
3. Normal safety comparisons failed.
4. The vehicle fell through to the route-waypoint assignment.
5. Multiple followers snapped to the same waypoint and stacked.

This happened on straight roads as well as turns because `noSmooth` belongs to
the upcoming route waypoint and can remain active over a long segment.

### Work completed

- Removed obsolete roundabout-clearance treatment from normal following logic.
- Made BRTs use the same following logic as ordinary vehicles.
- Added finite-number checks for speed, distance, position, and rotation.
- Invalid calculations now stop the affected vehicle instead of moving it.
- Added last-safe-position recovery.
- Added a regression check proving an invalid speed cannot cause waypoint
  teleportation.

## 5. Collision deletion and rear-end deadlocks

### Previous deletion behaviour

An hourly “tow-truck cleanup” deleted both members of every overlapping
civilian-vehicle pair inside signal-controlled intersections. This hid stacking
by making groups of cars disappear.

### Work completed

- Removed collision-based vehicle deletion completely.
- Vehicles now despawn only after completing a normal non-loop route at a map
  edge.
- Permanent BRT routes continue looping instead of despawning.

### Rear-end recovery

Previously, if a BRT and a leading car overlapped, every small movement by the
front car was rejected because it still overlapped the BRT. Both vehicles could
therefore remain stopped permanently.

Collision recovery now permits a movement only when it increases separation
from an existing overlap:

- The leading car may pull forward and escape.
- The following BRT may not push farther into the leading car.
- Once clear, both return to normal traffic behaviour.
- Neither vehicle is deleted or teleported.

A forced BRT/car overlap regression test confirms that the front car pulls
clear and the overlap resolves.

## 6. Traffic-light corrections

### Clinic T-junction at X18 Y6

Clinic Access is a one-tile vertical road centred on X18.5. The generic
two-lane signal helper had placed its controlled southbound lane at X17.45,
outside the traffic controller’s lateral tolerance.

The southbound approach now uses:

- Lane/stop X: `18.5`
- Stop-line Y: `6.95`
- Visible signal: retained in tile X17 Y6

Validation confirms that a southbound clinic car:

- Detects the correct signal
- Stops behind the line on red
- Proceeds through the junction on green

### 2×2 controlled-box rule

Every traffic light now controls the 2×2 road area centred on its junction.

A green light is permission to enter only when the controlled 2×2 box is
clear. The box check includes:

- Ordinary civilian vehicles
- Both BRTs
- The player vehicle

Rules:

- Red and non-committed yellow vehicles stop normally.
- A green vehicle waits behind its stop line while the box is occupied.
- A vehicle already past the stop line is allowed to finish clearing the
  junction.
- Traffic enters after the box becomes clear.

The configured box size is
`POPULATION_TRAFFIC_CONFIG.trafficLightControlledBoxTiles = 2`.

This is intended to move congestion into queues before intersections instead
of allowing vehicles to block the junction itself.

## 11. Important notes for the next developer

1. Reload the running game after traffic-system changes. Existing in-memory
   vehicles from an older broken version may retain corrupted state.
2. Do not reintroduce midpoint traffic seeding. Civilian traffic must enter
   only through the 15 edge gateways.
3. Do not delete vehicles as a collision-recovery strategy.
4. Do not add BRT-specific movement physics. BRT differences should remain
   limited to type, terminal spawn, permanent route, and layover schedule.
5. Preserve finite-number guards before all position assignments.
6. A vehicle already inside a signal box must be allowed to clear it; otherwise
   the 2×2 rule will create a permanent junction deadlock.
7. If traffic density is adjusted, rerun the one-hour overlap/finite-state
   stress simulation as well as the standard validators.
